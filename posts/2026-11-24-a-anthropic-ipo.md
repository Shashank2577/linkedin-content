## Post body (copy from here)

$119.5 billion.

That's the gross the Anthropic IPO and its concurrent private placement pulled in when the deal closed on November 20.

The details matter more than the headline. Anthropic sold 1,127,000,000 Class A shares at $83, including the full 147,000,000-share underwriter option. Gross IPO proceeds were about $93.5 billion. Anthropic received about $91.6 billion after the selling stockholders took their part.

Then came the private placement. It moved 313,253,007 Class N shares to AMD, Broadcom, Google, Micron, Microsoft, NVIDIA and TSMC for about $26 billion.

That last line is the one I'd bring to an architecture review. The companies selling Anthropic compute are now on its cap table. I've watched enterprise buyers miss that detail. It also means Anthropic has to publish quarterly numbers, file risk factors, and answer to public shareholders while it sells safety-critical infrastructure.

For buyers, the practical change is procurement. You can read the S-1 instead of a sales deck. You can see concentration risk, compute commitments, and how much revenue depends on a handful of customers. That's a different conversation.

A public vendor isn't automatically a safer vendor. But it is a more legible one. That's a real upgrade for anyone putting agents into production. I'd take it.
