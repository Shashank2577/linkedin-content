## Post body (copy from here)

Your vendor's license says no reverse engineering. An agent can now do in days what used to take years. What's your team's policy?

REA, the agent toolkit with over 330,000 stars, ships a disclaimer worth reading. It's for lawful research, education, interoperability, software preservation, and analysis of software you own or are authorized to examine. It also says cracked copies and license bypassing aren't authorized.

That puts the burden where it always was, on the person typing the prompt. What's changed is that the work is fast and cheap enough for a junior engineer to do it without noticing they crossed a line. Popcar's "years by hand, days with AI" is the same shift seen from the games side.

I see three reasonable policies. Ban it outright. Allow it for interoperability only, with legal sign-off first. Or allow it only for software you own. A fourth, "nobody decided", is the one I'd worry about.

I'm not a lawyer, and the rules differ by country. Which would your company pick?
