## Post body (copy from here)

Everyone is comparing models.

The teams that ship agents are comparing contracts.

The agent stack is a procurement stack. Control is where the run executes and who can stop it. Execution is which model handles each step and what happens when it fails. Memory is where the state lives and who can export it. Every one of those is a contract term before it's an architecture diagram.

Five launches this month made that obvious: Microsoft's MAI Licensed Weights puts the model inside your Azure tenant with your data and weights under your control, AWS Quick says data stays in your environment and doesn't train anyone else's models, and OpenClaw keeps state, memory and credentials on your hardware alongside 395,070 GitHub stars. ChatGPT Wallet keeps the card details away from the agent and lets Visa or Mastercard issue a token instead. Microsoft's 40-plus-company transparency push points at Sentinel and Purview, outside the model provider's logs.

Same pattern in all five. That's the pattern. The question isn't "which model is best." It's "where does the work run, who holds the evidence, and what happens when I leave."

I'd put those questions in the RFP before the benchmark table. A model that wins on score but loses on export rights is a worse vendor. I've seen platform decisions turn on that clause.

The hard part is that most procurement teams don't know to ask yet. That's your opening. Bring the questions, not the benchmark chart.
