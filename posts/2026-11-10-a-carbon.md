## Post body (copy from here)

The best agent demo this week didn't use one model. It used a swarm.

Google made Gemini 4 Carbon generally available yesterday. It's the frontier model for long-horizon work, and the launch is really about /swarm in Google Antigravity: one prompt becomes a coordinated team of agents in parallel isolated workspaces, with Carbon handling architecture, coordination and auditing, and cheaper models doing the execution and adversarial verification.

The benchmark claims are strong. Carbon ranks #1 on CursorBench 4.0, Perplexity's WANDR, and Zapier's AutomationBench at more than 2x lower cost per task than the next best external model. In the demo, seven public planning PDFs and 206 extracted engineering views became an interactive Three.js model of Google's Platform 37 office across 10 overnight loops.

Two things to hold onto. Carbon uses more tokens than Argon to explore and verify, so the sticker price is not the cost per task. And the introductory price is $2 per million input and $10 per million output only until November 30; after that it doubles to $4/$20.

The demo is flashy. The architecture is the signal: planner, workers, verifiers, isolated workspaces. That's what production agent teams will look like.
