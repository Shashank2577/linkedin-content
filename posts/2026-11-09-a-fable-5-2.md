## Post body (copy from here)

A team of 1,000 Claude agents formalized Fermat's Last Theorem in 30 hours.

Anthropic shipped Fable 5.2 and Mythos 5.2 yesterday. The headline numbers are real: up to 30% faster output, $8 per million input tokens, $40 per million output, and an optional fast mode at 2.5x for $16/$80.

The part I'd actually read is the multi-agent run. Anthropic gave the agents a list of 60 statements, a one-sentence sketch of the classical route, definitions, and 47 proved lemmas, then assigned lead and helper roles. It says it offered no other guidance on how to prove the theorem or organize themselves. They produced a proof seven times shorter than Anthropic's first formalization, using one-third the action tokens. Opus 5.5, with the same 1,000-agent setup, completed 26% in 18 hours and barely moved after 30.

Caveats: this is Anthropic's own evaluation, a single run per model, and the model is expensive. For most coding and knowledge work, Anthropic still recommends Opus 5.5 as the daily driver.

But the capability is no longer theoretical. Orchestrating a thousand agents is a feature you can buy. The question for architects is whether your harness can review what they produce.
