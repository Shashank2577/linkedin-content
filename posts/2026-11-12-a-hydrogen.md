## Post body (copy from here)

The frontier model vs the cheap execution model. The cheap one is winning more of the loop.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen costs $1.50 per million input tokens across a 1M-token window. Flat. Most models charge more as the context grows. Hydrogen is one price for the whole window. $1.50 input, $7.50 output, up to 1M tokens each way.

Google is positioning it as the everyday driver for agentic work rather than the frontier model, and it's 2.5x faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash at its default high thinking level. Different job. Terminal-Bench 4.1 goes from 18.2% to 57%. That's more than triple. Fabricated claims drop by more than 5x. And there are four thinking levels now, including a new xhigh tier.

Orchestrator in Google Antigravity is the part I'd actually test. Instead of picking one model for a session, it switches between Carbon and Hydrogen dynamically. Google says it reached near-Carbon task success on Terminal-Bench 4.0 while using 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster, with Carbon coordinating while Hydrogen powers the workers and verifiers. That's the trade.

I'd keep the caveats close. These are Google's benchmarks. Hydrogen isn't the model you use for the hardest architectural problem. But for the 80% of agent steps that are execution, the economics just changed.

The model war isn't only about the frontier anymore. It's about the cost of the middle.
