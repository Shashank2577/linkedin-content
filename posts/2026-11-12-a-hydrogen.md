## Post body (copy from here)

The frontier model vs the cheap execution model. The cheap one is winning more of the loop.

Gemini 4 Hydrogen costs $1.50 per million input tokens across a 1M-token window. Flat. Most models charge more as the context grows. Hydrogen is one price for the whole window, $1.50 input and $7.50 output, with up to 1M input and 1M output tokens.

Google is positioning it as the everyday driver for agentic work, not the frontier model. It's 2.5x faster than Gemini 3.8 Flash at its default high thinking level, triples Terminal-Bench 4.1 from 18.2% to 57%, and cuts fabricated claims by more than 5x. It also has four thinking levels, including a new xhigh tier.

The more interesting release is Orchestrator in Google Antigravity. Instead of picking one model for a session, it switches between Carbon and Hydrogen dynamically. Google says it reached near-Carbon task success on Terminal-Bench 4.0 while consuming 24% less quota and finishing 19% faster. Carbon can coordinate while Hydrogen powers workers and verifiers.

Caveat: these are Google's benchmarks, and Hydrogen is not the model you use for the hardest architectural problem. But for the 80% of agent steps that are execution, the economics just changed.

The model war is no longer only about the frontier. It's about the cost of the middle.
