## Post body (copy from here)

You're allowed to write pretty rough Python scripts and still understand how your tools work.

Ben Affleck describes writing "pretty shitty Python scripts" in a YouTube video that Simon Willison quoted. Then Affleck explains how convolutional neural networks get used in a visual effects pipeline. A tensor is a numerical version of an image: values for batch, frame, and the red, green and blue of every pixel. A CNN finds patterns, like the edge of a window ledge, so an artist can pull a green screen and drop in a new background faster.

That's a clear, usable explanation from someone who works with these tools for a living and doesn't call themselves an engineer.

What it says to me about hiring: the bar for fluency moved. People outside software now have working mental models of this stuff, and they got them from using it, not from a course. If your interview still tests whether a candidate can recite definitions, you're testing the part that's easiest to learn.

I'd rather ask what they'd do when the output looks right and is wrong.
