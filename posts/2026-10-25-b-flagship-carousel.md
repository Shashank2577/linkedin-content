SLIDE 1
5 things that follow when reverse engineering gets cheap.

SLIDE 2
1. Clones arrive in days.
PhotoCraft, a Photoshop-style app, reached 68,700 GitHub stars in 20 days. 72% of the released changes across the repos its org tracks disclose an AI agent.

SLIDE 3
2. The tools are free and everywhere.
REA: MIT licensed, over 330,000 stars, works with Claude Code, Codex, Cursor and Gemini CLI.

SLIDE 4
3. Communities strain.
decomp.dev's closing notice names hostile forks, rushed ports, and a progress number that became a leaderboard.

SLIDE 5
4. It's genuinely useful.
A scanner with no Linux driver: first scan within hours. A laptop webcam: brightness from 31.1 to 90.9 in a controlled low-light test. (Christian Schaller)

SLIDE 6
5. The tools can be attacked.
Hidden strings in a binary can mislead the LLM analyzing it. (arXiv 2605.30667)

SLIDE 7
What I'd do Monday:
1. Assume your binary gets read. Keep secrets and personal data out.
2. Decide a policy on reverse engineering your vendors.
3. Treat decompiler output as untrusted input.
