## Post body (copy from here)

948 working root exploits. $1,213,430 in API credits. One run.

OpenAI published the details yesterday. Its agents scanned about 2.8 million lines of the Linux kernel, roughly 7% of the codebase, and produced 2,308 candidate findings. They reproduced 1,724 in isolated VMs, then built 291 information-leak proofs of concept and 948 working local privilege escalation exploits. That's $1,279 per root exploit.

The target wasn't a weakened box. It was a stock desktop Linux install, with SMEP, SMAP, KASLR and HARDENED_USERCOPY on.

The part I'd actually read is what came out alongside each exploit: a root cause analysis, a crashing proof of concept, a candidate patch, and the exploit itself. A human researcher still reviews the bundle before anything goes to the maintainers. During the 10-day run, 61 of the bugs were patched upstream, 43 had staging patches, and 299 were being discussed publicly.

OpenAI's own framing is that discovery is no longer the bottleneck. Triage and patching are.

For anyone running infrastructure, that changes the priority. If a low-privileged account on an unpatched machine is functionally root for a well-resourced attacker, patch latency is the security control. Sandboxes and isolation limit the blast radius. They don't replace the patch.

The agents also used at least 10 exploitation techniques that OpenAI says were new to kernel experts. That's the line I'd sit with.
