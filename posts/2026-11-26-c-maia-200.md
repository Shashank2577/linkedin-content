## Post body (copy from here)

1.7x more tokens per watt.

That's Microsoft's claim for Maia 200, its second-generation AI accelerator, now running in Azure production.

The number matters because agent workloads are token-heavy and bursty. A chat completion is one request. An agent loop can be dozens of calls, each with a growing context. At that point, tokens per watt is a better capacity planning metric than peak FLOPS. That's the shift.

At Ignite, Microsoft said Maia 200 is now in production. The company says it delivers 1.7x more tokens per watt than the previous generation. It also says it plans multi-gigawatt Maia deployments, which is a supply-chain statement as much as a technical one. I've heard that before from chip teams.

I haven't seen an independent tokens-per-watt run, and the metric depends on model size, batch size and utilization. A chip can win on one workload and lose on another. That's the caveat.

The strategic read is simpler. Microsoft wants the inference layer it sells agents on to be its own silicon where possible, and NVIDIA where it isn't. For buyers, that means Azure capacity and pricing will increasingly depend on which model runs on which chip.

Ask your account team which of your production models are on Maia 200 today. That answer affects your unit economics more than the next benchmark. I'd ask this week.
