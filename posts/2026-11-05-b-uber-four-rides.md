## Post body (copy from here)

Four rides. 320 million trips and deliveries. One week.

On Wednesday's Q3 earnings call, Uber's CFO put a number on agentic commerce for the first time. Third-party AI agents fulfilled a grand total of four rides. During Halloween week they placed around 500 Uber Eats orders. Uber did about 320 million trips and deliveries that week.

One caveat: the transcript ties those numbers to a peak week, not the full quarter. The Verge reported it as a quarterly figure. Either way, the share is far below one percent.

The number is tiny for a boring reason. The app is already fast. Uber's CEO said the average ride booking takes 50 seconds, 75% of rides are a single tap, and reordering your usual food takes a median 90 seconds. An agent has to beat that experience, not beat a demo.

Uber also said it is negotiating terms with assistants and does not want to depend on any one player. That's the platform saying it will let agents in on its terms.

Four rides is one data point. It doesn't prove agents won't matter. It does show how much ground the demo still has to cover before it becomes the default.
