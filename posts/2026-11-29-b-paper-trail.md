## Post body (copy from here)

The biggest change in AI this year wasn't a model.

It was the paper trail.

A year ago, the agent conversation was capability. Can it browse, code, book, buy? This month the announcements were about evidence: OpenAI gave four outside labs access to source code, checkpoints and training data with the right to publish, Microsoft and 40-plus companies proposed an audit trail that lives outside the model provider's logs, and Google, OpenAI, Anthropic and Microsoft backed a shared robots.txt token that separates search from training. Microsoft said it will put licensed model weights inside a customer's Azure tenant. ChatGPT Wallet turned every agent purchase into a tokenized, biometrically approved event on Visa, Mastercard or Amex rails.

None of those is a model launch. All of them are about who can prove what happened. That's the shift.

This is what an institution looks like. Agents are moving from demos into payments, security operations, regulated data and public markets. Anthropic's IPO closing at a $119.5 billion gross raise is the same signal from the capital side. OpenClaw passed 395,070 GitHub stars in under a year on the same premise: state and credentials stay on your hardware. Once agents touch money, audits and shareholder risk, the demo stops being the product. The record becomes the product.

The honest caveat is that a paper trail is not a safety guarantee. It's voluntary in most of these cases, fragmented across vendors, and only as good as the people reading it. A log can be incomplete. An evaluator can be paid. A token can be ignored. I've seen all three.

Microsoft's transparency group points at Sentinel and Purview for a reason. But you can't audit what you can't see. This year the industry started building the seeing part.

I'd rather have an imperfect record than another benchmark chart. The record is the thing you can fix.
