## Post body (copy from here)

Writing code faster than your team can read it is quietly costing you the one skill that's still scarce.

Carson Gross, the htmx creator, put the job in two parts: solving problems with computers, and learning to control the complexity of the solutions. He doesn't expect the value of either to drop because of AI tools, so he thinks programming stays a viable career.

I agree with the premise and I'd push on the second half. AI tools make complexity cheaper to create. The control part didn't get cheaper. A feature that used to take a week and get read by three people can now take an afternoon and get skimmed by one.

The exit isn't to slow down. It's to set a limit on what the team accepts without a reader: a size cap on generated diffs, tests written before the code, and one person who can explain every module by name.

If nobody can explain it, you didn't ship a feature. You shipped a liability with a green build.
