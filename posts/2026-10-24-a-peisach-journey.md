## Post body (copy from here)

A decompilation can run and still not be understood. That's the part AI doesn't hand you.

Joshua Peisach, who has contributed nearly 200 functions to the LEGO Island decompilation, wrote a post this month about the race to decompile games with AI. Their point is that decompilation is valued for the process of understanding the code, not just the result. In their words: "It's not about the destination, it's about the journey."

Peisach isn't anti-AI. They say it can help experienced decompilers. What bothers them is people using agents to race to the first working port, sometimes by finishing other teams' public projects without contributing back.

The example that stuck with me: a Halo 2 decompilation they looked at, with over a thousand files in one folder, many placeholder names like unknown_ and function_, and structs where C++ classes would fit better. It's the engineering version of code that passes the demo and that nobody can maintain.

If your team ships agent-written code, "it runs" is the start of the review, not the end.
