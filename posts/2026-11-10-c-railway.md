## Post body (copy from here)

Everyone thinks a breach is about customer data. Railway's attacker was hunting AI provider keys.

Railway published the full postmortem of its October 21 incident yesterday. The short version: a previously unknown vulnerability let the attacker partially escape build isolation, steal a build credential, read stored histories of 353 builds, find an OIDC misconfiguration, and eventually decrypt environment variables for at most 1.85% of workspaces, about 100,000.

The forensic detail that matters: the attacker queried variable names with regular expressions specifically matching AI provider keys, and prioritized customers with lots of variables. Railway believes the keys were passed to distributed transfer stations for distillation. Unauthorized spend on stolen keys is estimated at ~$740,000. The attacker also read linked-account tokens for 1,852 users, which Railway reset.

Railway's containment was aggressive: builds halted twice, all user sessions and tokens invalidated, every relevant key rotated, a new root CA, and internal systems rebuilt from validated source. It also says it had a new VM-isolated build system ready for a limited rollout the week after the attack, and accelerated it to everyone.

The lesson is not "use Railway." It's that AI keys are now the thing attackers scan for. Treat them like production database credentials: short-lived, scoped, rotated, and never sitting in a build cache.
