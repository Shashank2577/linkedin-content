## Post body (copy from here)

Everyone thinks a breach is about customer data. Railway's attacker was hunting AI provider keys.

Railway published the full postmortem of its October 21 incident yesterday. The short version: a previously unknown vulnerability let the attacker partially escape build isolation, steal a build credential, read stored histories of 353 builds, find an OIDC misconfiguration, and eventually decrypt environment variables for at most 1.85% of workspaces. That's about 100,000.

Here's the detail that got me. The attacker queried variable names with regular expressions that specifically matched AI provider keys. They prioritized customers with lots of variables. Railway believes the keys were passed to distributed transfer stations for distillation. Unauthorized spend on stolen keys is estimated at ~$740,000. The attacker also read linked-account tokens for 1,852 users; Railway reset them.

Railway's containment was aggressive. It halted builds twice, invalidated every user session and token, rotated every relevant key, swapped in a new root CA, and rebuilt internal systems from validated source. It had a VM-isolated build system ready for a limited rollout the week after the attack. It accelerated that to everyone.

I wouldn't take the lesson as "use Railway." I'd take it as: AI keys are now the thing attackers scan for. Treat them like production database credentials. Short-lived, scoped, rotated, and never sitting in a build cache.
