## Post body (copy from here)

Reverse engineered, cloned and clean-room are three different claims. The coverage this month treats them as one.

Tom's Hardware's headlines describe free Office-style apps as "replications" and the earlier Adobe-style apps as rebuilt, and say it "targets 100 percent parity in one month." Hacker News commenters argued over whether "reverse engineered" is even the right label. The ArtCraft site itself says none of it: no parity claim, no clean-room claim, just free and open source.

Here's what the words usually mean. Reverse engineering is studying how software behaves. A clean-room reimplementation traditionally has one team study the original and write a spec, and a separate team build from the spec only. Cloning is copying what it does or looks like. One Hacker News commenter pointed out that an agent can now do the spec step by clicking through a UI, with no decompiler at all.

On the legal side, attorney Michael Wang, quoted by 404 Media, put it simply: ideas can't be copyrighted or patented, exact copies of code and assets are clear violations, and similar ideas often aren't. I'm not a lawyer.

If you're evaluating one of these tools for a client, ask which of the three it is. The vendor's answer matters more than the star count.
