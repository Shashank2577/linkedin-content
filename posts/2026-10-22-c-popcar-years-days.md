## Post body (copy from here)

Years by hand. Days with AI. A 2011 Flash game that was locked to 30 FPS now runs at 165.

That's from Popcar, an indie game developer who says up front they dislike generative AI. Their post, "AI is mass decompiling games," lists what changed. Decompilation that once took years of volunteer work can now take days, "or maybe less." The Binding of Isaac's original Flash version has been ported to C++ and runs at native 1440p, interpolated to 165 FPS. There are hundreds of such projects, and old games can finally get ports, mods and a life after the servers shut down.

Then Popcar lists the risks, and they're the ones I'd put in front of a product team: software copied or open-sourced against its owner's will, competitors reading each other's code, open-source licenses getting harder to enforce, and companies responding with heavier DRM. Popcar expects major applications to be decompiled within the next few months.

I like that the post holds both. You can think this is wonderful for preservation and a problem for your product at the same time.
